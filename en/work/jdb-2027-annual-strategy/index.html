Overview

JDB’s 2027 planning required one brand to solve several different growth tasks at once: reinforce the relevance of the classic Red Can, establish a more everyday role for the herbal product line, and explore a culturally meaningful collaboration around regional heritage. Our proposal built an integrated communication framework that gave each workstream a distinct role while keeping them connected to the same brand equity.

01The strategic challenge

One brand. Different reasons to choose it.

Classic Red Can
Bring a familiar function into real-life triggers, earlier in the decision.
Herbal Product Line
Remove the perceived taste compromise and build an everyday reason to choose it.
Cultural Collaboration
Move heritage beyond decoration into sound and participation.

02Annual strategy

Give each product line a distinct job within shared brand equity — rather than a collection of disconnected campaigns.

From brand memory to everyday choice.

03Classic Red Can

The classic Red Can already carried strong functional memory. The task was to move that memory one step forward: from recall after discomfort to a choice made before a high-intensity meal, emotional stress or a late night begins.

Life heats up. Choose JDB first.

04Herbal Product Line

Herbal credentials can suggest health, but also bitterness, medicinal taste or compromise. The strategy made taste the first invitation: younger consumers need a reason to try that first sip, so herbal health and enjoyable drinking no longer compete.

Healthy should not mean compromising on taste.

05Cultural Collaboration

The proposal explored how Zhuang embroidery and Guangxi’s Sanyuesan context could move beyond static heritage display. Connecting visual craft with sound, limited-edition product directions and cultural content would give the brand a role as a bridge between local heritage and younger audiences.

From seeing the embroidery to hearing the embroidery.

06My contribution
